How it works

  1. Tell us the basics. Undergraduate, master’s or PhD; your start and hand-in dates; any deadlines your university has set (proposal, ethics, full draft, upgrade, viva); and the kind of research you’re doing.
  2. Get a week-by-week plan. Trawpie spreads the work across the time you have, with about 10% spare before hand-in: topic and proposal, ethics if you need it, literature review, methods, data or building, analysis, writing, revisions and formatting. Every task has a date and a short checklist.
  3. Keep going. Tick off tasks each week, update your word counts, and let anything unfinished carry into the next week on its own. If a task keeps slipping, you’ll see it long before the deadline does.

For undergraduate, master’s and PhD

Undergraduate

Final-year dissertations and projects, usually over your final year.

  • Proposal and supervisor meetings first
  • A 10,000-word structure you can change
  • Engineering projects get Design, Implementation and Testing chapters

Master’s

Often one busy summer, or a year part-time. The plan fits whichever you have.

  • A tighter timeline with a full draft about 80% of the way in
  • 15,000 words by default
  • Ethics, data and analysis scheduled around it

PhD

Three or four years, planned as phases with weekly tasks as you go.

  • Upgrade or confirmation review, annual reviews
  • Studies, papers and conferences
  • Writing up, submission, the viva and corrections

Everything a dissertation needs, in one place

Chapters and word counts

Each chapter has a target, a status (drafting, with your supervisor, revising, final) and the words you’ve written. It’s not about counting every word; it’s about knowing where you are.

A proposal helper that leaves the writing to you

A proposal answers the same few questions in every subject: what, why, the gap, your question, your aims, your methods, ethics and timeline. Trawpie walks you through them and gives gentle hints, like whether your question can be answered with a yes or no, or whether your objectives start with a verb. Then it puts your words into an outline you can paste into your university’s form.

Supervisor meetings that turn into actions

Before each meeting, note your progress and questions; afterwards, the next steps you agreed become tasks with dates. Invite your supervisor and they can see your plan and progress and comment, without being able to change anything.

Reading, checklists and the rest

  • Reading list: add papers by DOI, track what you’ve read, and export BibTeX for LaTeX, Zotero or Mendeley.
  • Checklists: ethics, everything to check before you hand in, and viva preparation.
  • Lab notebook: dated notes and photos on any task, handy for experiments and builds.
  • Calendar: put your deadlines in Google Calendar or Outlook.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. Planning your dissertation or thesis in Trawpie is free, and students get Pro Education free too.

Does it write my dissertation?

No. It helps you plan, track and keep going. Every word is yours, which is exactly what your university expects.

Can my supervisor see my plan?

Only if you invite them. A supervisor sees your plan, chapters and progress and can comment, but can’t change anything. You can also give them tasks, like “Read chapter 2”.

Does it work for a PhD?

Yes. A PhD plan covers all three or four years in phases: the literature review and research questions, training, ethics, a pilot, the upgrade or confirmation review, your studies, papers and conferences, annual reviews, writing up, submission, the viva and corrections. The weeks on the board start with your first term and grow as you go.

What if my deadlines change?

Change the dates and the plan updates. Your word counts and ticked checklists are kept, and unfinished tasks carry into the next week on their own.

Can I use my department’s chapter structure?

Yes. Rename, add or remove chapters and set your own word targets. Engineering final-year projects start with Design, Implementation and Testing chapters; literature-based dissertations start with themes instead of results.
